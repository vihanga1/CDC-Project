Projects & Programs

Long-term work with communities, land, and local systems

CDC's projects grow out of real community needs in Sri Lanka. Over the years, the organisation has worked with local people and a range of partners on conservation, livelihoods, women empowerment, water access, agriculture, and post-disaster support.

A practical view of the work

  • Community-grounded themes rather than a long report
  • Selected project examples and partner references
  • Focus on continuity, local engagement, and trust

Where our work makes a difference

CDC's work moves across a few connected themes

Environment & Conservation

Biodiversity, reforestation, and care for fragile landscapes

CDC has worked on environmental conservation, biodiversity protection, and land stewardship, including areas connected to Knuckles and Ududumbara. Indigenous yam conservation is part of this wider effort to protect local ecological knowledge.

Livelihoods & Agriculture

Support for farming, food security, and household income

A significant part of CDC's project work connects to agriculture, food security, and livelihood improvement. This includes work that helps rural communities strengthen production, local enterprise, and more stable ways of earning.

Women & Community Development

Leadership, social integration, and practical community support

CDC has worked with women, families, and community groups on empowerment, leadership, and social development. These programs are closely tied to local participation rather than short-term activity alone.

Water, Basic Needs & Recovery

Essential support in everyday conditions and difficult moments

Some projects have focused on water supply, basic community needs, and post-disaster rehabilitation. This reflects CDC's practical role in responding where communities need support to rebuild and continue.

Working with partners

CDC's work has been strengthened through collaboration

Over time, CDC has worked with local and international partners on a wide range of community-based programs. These partnerships help connect local experience with wider support, while keeping the work grounded in community realities.

Long-term commitment

CDC's work is built on continuity rather than one-off activity

Across different projects and partnerships, the organisation keeps returning to the same foundation: working with communities over time, earning trust through local engagement, and staying close to the realities people face. That continuity matters as much as any single program.
