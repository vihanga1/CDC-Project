Water Access & Infrastructure

Hing Patuna Phase 2 Water Supply Project

Phase 2 of the Hing Patuna water supply project extended clean drinking water access to remote communities in Geradigala and Munamalapelessa — areas that had long relied on difficult or unsafe water sources — through CDC guidance and sustained community participation.

Geradigala • Munamalapelessa

About this project

Reaching further into remote communities with clean water

Access to clean water is one of the most basic requirements for a healthy and productive life. In highland communities such as Geradigala and Munamalapelessa, reaching reliable water sources has historically involved significant effort and risk. The Hing Patuna Phase 2 Water Supply Project built on earlier infrastructure work to extend piped water access further into these communities, reducing the burden on households — especially women and children — and improving the quality and safety of daily water use. CDC guided the planning and community coordination process throughout, ensuring that the communities themselves were central to how the infrastructure was designed and managed.

Why It Matters

Clean Water Changes Everything

Access to clean water is not a single benefit — it is a multiplier. When families have reliable water at home, children attend school more consistently, women have more time for other productive activities, health outcomes improve, and communities can plan with greater confidence. The Hing Patuna project represents that kind of compounded, lasting change.

  • Extended clean piped water access to Geradigala and Munamalapelessa communities
  • Reduced burden on women and children who previously travelled to collect water
  • Improved health conditions through safer household water sources
  • Community participation in planning and managing water infrastructure
